Armoured vs Unarmoured Cable: Which One Does Your Project Need?

One of the most common questions we receive from procurement teams is whether they need armoured or unarmoured cable for their installation. The answer depends on where and how the cable will be installed. This guide breaks down the differences and helps you make an informed decision.

What Is Armoured Cable?

Armoured cable has a protective metal layer — typically steel wire or steel tape — wrapped around the inner sheath. This layer provides mechanical protection against external forces: crushing, impact, rodent damage, and tensile stress during installation. The armour also serves as the circuit protective conductor (earth) in multi-core SWA cables, eliminating the need for a separate earth conductor.

The armour layer sits between the inner bedding sheath and the outer PVC or LSZH oversheath. In cross-section, you can clearly identify the galvanized steel wires spiralling around the cable core.

Types of Armoured Cable

SWA — Steel Wire Armour

SWA is the most common armoured cable specification worldwide, manufactured to IEC 60502, BS 5467, and equivalent standards. It uses galvanized steel wires laid helically over the inner sheath. SWA is suitable for:

  • Direct burial underground without additional conduit
  • External wall mounting and outdoor cable runs
  • Industrial environments with risk of mechanical impact
  • Areas with rodent activity
  • Cable tray installations where additional protection is specified

SWA is rated for both LV (0.6/1kV) and MV (up to 35kV) applications, with conductor counts from 2 to 5 cores plus armour as earth.

AWA — Aluminium Wire Armour

AWA uses aluminium wires instead of steel. It's specifically designed for single-core AC cables. Why? In a single-core cable carrying AC current, a steel armour would form a closed magnetic circuit around the conductor, inducing eddy currents that cause heating and power loss. Aluminium, being non-magnetic, avoids this problem. AWA is lighter than SWA and is the standard choice for single-core MV power distribution.

STA — Steel Tape Armour

Less common in export markets but widely used in China (per GB/T 12706), STA uses steel tapes wound around the cable instead of wires. It provides good radial protection against penetration but is less flexible than SWA during installation.

When to Choose Unarmoured Cable

Unarmoured cable has no metal protection layer — it consists of conductors, insulation, bedding (if multi-core), and an outer sheath. It's lighter, more flexible, easier to terminate, and significantly cheaper. Unarmoured cable is suitable for:

  • Indoor installations within conduit or trunking
  • Cable tray runs in protected environments
  • Flexible connections to equipment and machinery
  • Temporary installations and event power
  • Overhead ABC (Aerial Bundled Cable) where the messenger wire provides mechanical support

Unarmoured cable should not be direct-buried or installed where it could be struck, crushed, or chewed by rodents.

Cost Comparison

Armoured cable typically costs 25-50% more than the equivalent unarmoured specification, depending on conductor size and armour type. The added cost is driven by: galvanized steel/aluminium material, additional manufacturing steps, higher shipping weight, and larger drum requirements due to the increased bending radius. However, the cost must be weighed against the alternative — if you use unarmoured cable underground, you'll need to install and maintain protective conduit, which can easily exceed the cost of SWA.

How to Decide: A Simple Framework

Ask these four questions:

  1. Will it be buried? Yes → SWA or AWA. No → check next question.
  2. Is it exposed to potential impact or damage? Yes → armoured. No → check next question.
  3. Is it a single-core AC cable? Yes → AWA if armoured, or unarmoured with mechanical protection. No (multi-core) → SWA.
  4. Is the installation in a fully protected indoor environment? Yes → unarmoured is likely fine. No → use armoured.

Common Installation Mistakes

Three things we see go wrong in the field: (1) Using unarmoured cable for direct burial — the sheath gets damaged within months. (2) Not properly glanding and earthing the armour at both ends of an SWA run — this compromises both mechanical security and electrical safety. (3) Bending armoured cable below its minimum bend radius (typically 8-12× the cable outer diameter) during installation, which can kink the armour and damage the underlying insulation.
