How to Choose the Right Power Cable: A Complete Buyer's Guide

Selecting the right power cable for your project is one of the most critical procurement decisions you'll make. Choose wrong, and you risk project delays, safety hazards, and unexpected costs. This guide walks you through the key factors every buyer should evaluate — from voltage rating and conductor material to insulation type and armouring.

1. Determine Your Voltage Requirements

Power cables are broadly classified by voltage grade. The two most common categories in industrial and commercial applications are:

  • Low Voltage (LV): Rated 0.6/1kV — used in building wiring, machinery, and indoor distribution. These are the workhorses of construction and industrial facilities.
  • Medium Voltage (MV): Rated 6/10kV to 26/35kV — used for primary distribution networks, substation interconnections, and large industrial plants where power must travel longer distances.

The voltage rating you need is primarily determined by your local grid standard and the distance between your power source and the load. If you're unsure, consult your electrical engineer — specifying the wrong voltage grade can lead to insulation breakdown and catastrophic failure.

2. Choose Your Conductor Material: Copper or Aluminium

This is the single biggest cost driver in cable procurement. Both materials have their place:

  • Copper: Higher conductivity (58 MS/m), smaller cross-section for the same current, more flexible, better corrosion resistance. Ideal for confined spaces, marine environments, and applications where reliability is paramount. Approximately 3× the price of aluminium.
  • Aluminium: Lower conductivity (36 MS/m) but significantly lighter (70% less weight) and cheaper. Requires a larger cross-section for the same current rating. Widely used in overhead transmission and large-scale distribution where weight and cost savings justify the larger conductor size.

For most B2B procurement scenarios, the choice comes down to: budget (aluminium wins), space constraints (copper wins), and installation environment.

3. Select the Right Insulation Material

The insulation system determines the cable's maximum operating temperature, short-circuit rating, and environmental resistance:

  • PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride): Cost-effective, rated up to 70°C. Good for general-purpose LV applications. Flame-retardant grades available.
  • XLPE (Cross-Linked Polyethylene): Rated up to 90°C, higher current-carrying capacity than PVC for the same cross-section. The standard choice for MV cables and modern LV installations. Better moisture and chemical resistance.
  • EPR (Ethylene Propylene Rubber): Excellent flexibility, heat resistance, and ozone resistance. Commonly used in mining, marine, and flexible cable applications.
  • LSZH (Low Smoke Zero Halogen): Mandatory for enclosed public spaces (tunnels, metro, hospitals). Emits minimal smoke and no toxic halogens in a fire.

4. Armoured or Unarmoured?

If your cable will be buried underground, exposed to mechanical stress, or installed where rodents are a concern, you need armoured cable. The two most common types:

  • SWA (Steel Wire Armour): Galvanized steel wires wound around the inner sheath. Provides excellent mechanical protection. The standard for underground LV/MV distribution.
  • AWA (Aluminium Wire Armour): Used with single-core AC cables where steel armour would induce eddy currents. Lighter than SWA.
  • Unarmoured: Suitable for indoor installations in conduit or cable tray, where mechanical damage risk is minimal.

5. Confirm Core Count and Cross-Section

The number of cores depends on your power system: single-phase typically uses 2 or 3 cores; three-phase uses 3, 4 (3 phases + neutral), or 5 cores (3P+N+earth). Cross-sectional area (mm²) is determined by the load current, voltage drop over distance, and installation conditions (ambient temperature, grouping). Always have an engineer size the cable per IEC 60364 or your local equivalent.

6. Check Compliance and Certifications

Before placing an order, verify the manufacturer can provide:

  • Applicable IEC, BS, or GB/T test certificates
  • Factory inspection reports and mill certificates for the copper/aluminium
  • Third-party testing (SGS, BV, TUV) if required by your contract
  • ISO 9001 quality management certification

At Amperon Cables, every shipment includes full documentation — datasheets, test reports, and a packing list with drum-level traceability.

7. Plan Your Logistics

Power cables are heavy and shipped on wooden or steel drums. Before you finalize your order, consider: drum dimensions and container utilization, port of destination and Incoterms, customs documentation (Form E, certificate of origin), and whether you need pre-shipment inspection. A well-planned shipment saves thousands in demurrage and handling fees.

Key Takeaway

The right cable choice balances technical requirements, budget, and installation environment. When in doubt, share your project specifications with the manufacturer — a good supplier will guide you toward the optimal specification rather than just taking your order.
