Power Distribution Cable Overview

Power distribution networks form the critical link between generation and end-user — moving electricity from transmission substations through primary (MV) and secondary (LV) distribution to homes, businesses, and industrial facilities. Amperon manufactures the full range of cables required for these networks: MV primary distribution cables for underground and overhead circuits, LV secondary distribution cables for street-level and rural reticulation, aerial bundled conductors (ABC) for overhead LV, and service drop cables for the final connection to the consumer.

Whether you are a utility planning a grid extension, an EPC contractor building a new substation, or a rural electrification agency rolling out last-mile connections, we can supply cables to IEC, BS, and GB/T standards with aluminium or copper conductors in the cross-sections and drum lengths your project demands.

Key Distribution Network Design Considerations

FactorGuidance
Voltage Drop LimitsUtilities typically limit voltage drop to 5-6% from source to consumer. For long rural feeders, aluminium conductor with larger cross-section is the cost-effective solution. We provide R and X values for accurate voltage drop calculation.
Short-Circuit RatingDistribution cables must withstand prospective fault current for the protection clearing time. MV cables are rated for 1-second short-circuit temperatures of 250°C (XLPE). We can verify your cable size against network fault levels.
Laying MethodDirect burial (armoured, with sand bedding and cable tiles), ducted (unarmoured or armoured, pulled through HDPE ducts), overhead (ABC on poles), or in cable trench (on racks). Laying method affects ampacity derating.
Phase IdentificationIEC colour coding (brown/black/grey), BS (brown/black/grey), or utility-specific conventions. Outer sheath can be red (MV, warning) or black (LV, UV-stable) per project specification.
Concentric NeutralLV distribution cables frequently use a concentric neutral layer of copper wires wrapped around the cores, providing both neutral and earth continuity while simplifying jointing.

Distribution Network Applications

  • Primary MV feeders — XLPE-insulated single-core or three-core MV cables connecting primary substations to distribution substations in urban and industrial networks
  • Ring main circuits — MV cables forming closed-loop distribution rings with RMUs (Ring Main Units) for supply redundancy and fault isolation
  • Rural electrification — overhead ABC on wooden or concrete poles for cost-effective LV distribution over long distances in rural areas
  • Urban underground distribution — SWA armoured LV and MV cables laid in ducts or directly buried beneath streets in city distribution networks
  • Substation internal cabling — MV and LV power cables for transformer connections, switchgear interconnections, and auxiliary supply within substation compounds
  • Street lighting circuits — concentric or ABC cables for series-connected street lighting on main roads, residential streets, and public spaces
